## Lilacs / Lilacs
- Theme: Plant
- Author: Amy Lowell
- Author Years: 1874–1925
- Public Domain Reason: 作者は1925年に没しており、著作権保護期間（死後70年）は満了。本作も1920年に初出、1925年刊の詩集『What's O'Clock』所収で、1929年以前の米国刊行物としてパブリックドメイン。
- Source URL: https://www.best-poems.net/amy-lowell/lilacs.html
- Added Date: 2026-07-14

### 原文
Lilacs,
False blue,
White,
Purple,
Color of lilac,
Your great puffs of flowers
Are everywhere in this my New England.
Among your heart-shaped leaves
Orange orioles hop like music-box birds and sing
Their little weak soft songs;
In the crooks of your branches
The bright eyes of song sparrows sitting on spotted eggs
Peer restlessly through the light and shadow
Of all Springs.
Lilacs in dooryards
Holding quiet conversations with an early moon;
Lilacs watching a deserted house
Settling sideways into the grass of an old road;
Lilacs, wind-beaten, staggering under a lopsided shock of bloom
Above a cellar dug into a hill.
You are everywhere.
You were everywhere.
You tapped the window when the preacher preached his sermon,
And ran along the road beside the boy going to school.
You stood by the pasture-bars to give the cows good milking,
You persuaded the housewife that her dishpan was of silver.
And her husband an image of pure gold.
You flaunted the fragrance of your blossoms
Through the wide doors of Custom Houses—
You, and sandal-wood, and tea,
Charging the noses of quill-driving clerks
When a ship was in from China.
You called to them: "Goose-quill men, goose-quill men,
May is a month for flitting."
Until they writhed on their high stools
And wrote poetry on their letter-sheets behind the propped-up ledgers.
Paradoxical New England clerks,
Writing inventories in ledgers, reading the "Song of Solomon" at night,
So many verses before bed-time,
Because it was the Bible.
The dead fed you
Amid the slant stones of graveyards.
Pale ghosts who planted you
Came in the nighttime
And let their thin hair blow through your clustered stems.
You are of the green sea,
And of the stone hills which reach a long distance.
You are of elm-shaded streets with little shops where they sell kites and marbles,
You are of great parks where every one walks and nobody is at home.
You cover the blind sides of greenhouses
And lean over the top to say a hurry-word through the glass
To your friends, the grapes, inside.
Lilacs,
False blue,
White,
Purple,
Color of lilac,
You have forgotten your Eastern origin,
The veiled women with eyes like panthers,
The swollen, aggressive turbans of jeweled pashas.
Now you are a very decent flower,
A reticent flower,
A curiously clear-cut, candid flower,
Standing beside clean doorways,
Friendly to a house-cat and a pair of spectacles,
Making poetry out of a bit of moonlight
And a hundred or two sharp blossoms.
Maine knows you,
Has for years and years;
New Hampshire knows you,
And Massachusetts
And Vermont.
Cape Cod starts you along the beaches to Rhode Island;
Connecticut takes you from a river to the sea.
You are brighter than apples,
Sweeter than tulips,
You are the great flood of our souls
Bursting above the leaf-shapes of our hearts,
You are the smell of all Summers,
The love of wives and children,
The recollection of gardens of little children,
You are State Houses and Charters
And the familiar treading of the foot to and fro on a road it knows.
May is lilac here in New England,
May is a thrush singing "Sun up!" on a tip-top ash tree,
May is white clouds behind pine-trees
Puffed out and marching upon a blue sky.
May is a green as no other,
May is much sun through small leaves,
May is soft earth,
And apple-blossoms,
And windows open to a South Wind.
May is full light wind of lilac
From Canada to Narragansett Bay.
Lilacs,
False blue,
White,
Purple,
Color of lilac.
Heart-leaves of lilac all over New England,
Roots of lilac under all the soil of New England,
Lilac in me because I am New England,
Because my roots are in it,
Because my leaves are of it,
Because my flowers are for it,
Because it is my country
And I speak to it of itself
And sing of it with my own voice
Since certainly it is mine.

## A Field Flower / A Field Flower
- Theme: Plant
- Author: James Montgomery
- Author Years: 1771–1854
- Public Domain Reason: 作者は1854年に没しており、著作権保護期間（死後70年）はとうに満了。19世紀前半の作品でパブリックドメイン。
- Source URL: https://allpoetry.com/The-Daisy---On-Finding-one-in-Bloom-on-Christmas-day
- Added Date: 2026-07-14

### 原文
There is a flower, a little flower
With silver crest and golden eye,
That welcomes every changing hour,
And weathers every sky.

The prouder beauties of the field
In gay but quick succession shine;
Race after race their honors yield,
They flourish and decline.

But this small flower, to Nature dear,
While moons and stars their courses run,
Wreathes the whole circle of the year,
Companion of the Sun.

It smiles upon the lap of May,
To sultry August spreads its charms,
Lights pale October on his way,
And twines December's arms.

The purple heath and golden broom
On moory mountains catch the gale;
O'er lawns the lily sheds perfume,
The violet in the vale.

But this bold floweret climbs the hill,
Hides in the forest, haunts the glen,
Plays on the margin of the rill,
Peeps round the fox's den.

Within the garden's cultured round
It shares the sweet carnation's bed;
And blooms on consecrated ground
In honor of the dead.

The lambkin crops its crimson gem;
The wild bee murmurs on its breast;
The blue-fly bends its pensile stem
Light o'er the skylark's nest.

'Tis Flora's page, - in every place,
In every season, fresh and fair;
It opens with perennial grace,
And blossoms everywhere.

On waste and woodland, rock and plain,
Its humble buds unheeded rise;
The Rose has but a summer reign;
The Daisy never dies!

### 日本語訳
一輪の花がある、小さな花が——
銀の冠と金の瞳をもち、
移りゆくどの時をも迎え入れ、
どんな空にも耐え抜く花が。

野の、もっと誇らかな美しい花々は
陽気に、しかしまたたく間に咲き継いで輝き、
種を継ぐごとに、その栄えを譲り渡し、
咲き誇っては、やがて衰えてゆく。

けれどこの小さな花は、自然にとって愛しく、
月や星がそのめぐりを続けるあいだ、
一年のめぐりの輪をまるごと花輪に編み、
太陽の伴侶となる。

それは五月の膝の上でほほえみ、
蒸し暑い八月にはその魅力をひろげ、
青ざめた十月の道を照らし、
十二月の腕にからみつく。

紫のヒースと金のエニシダは
沼地の山で疾風をとらえ、
芝生の上ではユリが香りをこぼし、
谷にはスミレが咲く。

けれどこの大胆な小花は丘を登り、
森に隠れ、峡谷にひそみ、
小川のふちで戯れ、
狐の巣穴のまわりをのぞく。

庭の手入れされた花壇のうちでは
甘いカーネーションの床を分かち合い、
また聖別された地にも咲く——
死者を悼んで。

子羊はその深紅の宝玉を食（は）み、
野の蜂はその胸の上でうなり、
青蠅はその垂れ下がる茎を、
ヒバリの巣の上に軽くたわめる。

それはフローラの侍童——どの場所でも、
どの季節でも、みずみずしく美しく、
たえまない優雅さとともに花を開き、
どこにでも咲きこぼれる。

荒地にも森にも、岩にも平原にも、
その慎ましい蕾は人知れず立ち上がる。
バラの治世はただ夏のあいだだけ——
だがデイジーは、けっして死なない！

### 解説
主題は、ひなぎく（デイジー）という野の小さな花を讃え、その慎ましさと不屈の生命力を、華やかだが儚い他の花々と対比して描くことにある。植物テーマとの関係はきわめて明快で、詩全体が一輪のデイジーの生態と象徴性をめぐって展開する。副題「クリスマスの日に一輪咲いているのを見つけて」が示すように、真冬にまで咲くこの花の耐久性が着想の核である。印象的なのは、「銀の冠と金の瞳」という擬人的な描写、四季をめぐって咲き続ける様子を「太陽の伴侶」と讃える表現、そして庭・荒地・墓地までも咲く場所として列挙する視野の広がりである。読みどころは、"The Rose has but a summer reign; / The Daisy never dies!" という結びの対句で、豪奢な美の一過性に対して、目立たぬものの持続する生命を高らかに肯定するところにある。

## The Flowers / The Flowers
- Theme: Plant
- Author: Robert Louis Stevenson
- Author Years: 1850–1894
- Public Domain Reason: 作者は1894年に没しており、著作権保護期間（死後70年）はとうに満了。本作も1885年刊の詩集『A Child's Garden of Verses』所収でパブリックドメイン。
- Source URL: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Child%27s_Garden_of_Verses/The_Flowers
- Added Date: 2026-07-14

### 原文
All the names I know from nurse:
Gardener's garters, Shepherd's purse,
Bachelor's buttons, Lady's smock,
And the Lady Hollyhock.

Fairy places, fairy things,
Fairy woods where the wild bee wings,
Tiny trees for tiny dames—
These must all be fairy names!

Tiny woods below whose boughs
Shady fairies weave a house;
Tiny tree-tops, rose or thyme,
Where the braver fairies climb!

Fair are grown-up people's trees,
But the fairest woods are these;
Where, if I were not so tall,
I should live for good and all.

### 日本語訳
乳母から教わった名前はみんな——
ガードナーズ・ガーターズ、シェパーズ・パース、
バチェラーズ・ボタン、レディーズ・スモック、
それにレディー・ホリーホック。

妖精の場所、妖精のものたち、
野の蜂が翅を鳴らす妖精の森、
小さな貴婦人たちのための小さな木々——
これはみんな、きっと妖精の名前にちがいない！

小さな森、その枝々の下では
木陰の妖精たちが家を編み、
小さな梢、バラやタイムの上を、
勇敢な妖精たちがよじ登る！

大人たちの木々も美しいけれど、
いちばん美しい森はこれ。
もし私がこんなに背が高くなかったら、
ここにずっと、いつまでも住むのに。

### 解説
主題は、子どもの目に映った草花の世界を、その名前の響きの楽しさとともに妖精の国として空想する童心の情景である。植物テーマとの関係は明快で、ガードナーズ・ガーターズ（イワミツバ）やシェパーズ・パース（ナズナ）、バチェラーズ・ボタン、レディーズ・スモック、タチアオイといった草花の英名が次々に呼び上げられ、それらが小さな「妖精の森」を形づくる。印象的なのは、素朴な花の名を並べるだけで別世界を立ち上げる言葉遊びの喜びと、低い草花を見おろすのではなく、その高さまで身を縮めて入り込みたいと願う、子ども特有の縮尺の逆転である。読みどころは、「もし私がこんなに背が高くなかったら、ここにずっと住むのに」という結びで、成長によって失われてゆく小さきものへの親密さを、惜しむように優しくうたい上げている点にある。
